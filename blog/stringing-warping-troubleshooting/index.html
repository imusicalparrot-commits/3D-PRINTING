Stringing and Warping: A Practical Troubleshooting Guide

2026-09-046 min read

Two failure modes cause most ugly prints, and each has a surprisingly short culprit list. Match the symptom, apply one fix, reprint. No ritual sacrifices required.

Stringing: the hairy print

Fine plastic threads between separate parts of the model mean the nozzle is leaking while traveling. Fixes, in order:

  • Dry the filament. Damp plastic bubbles and oozes — this one cause explains half of all stringing. See the storage guide.
  • Retraction distance and speed. Increase distance in 0.1 mm steps (direct drive) or 0.5 mm steps (bowden) until the hairs thin out.
  • Temperature down 5°C. Cooler melt oozes less. Stop when corners start rounding.

Warping: the curling corner

Corners lifting off the bed mean the part is shrinking faster than it is sticking. Fixes:

  • Kill the draft. A window two meters away is enough. Enclosures solve this completely for ABS/ASA.
  • Brim, not raft. A 5 mm brim anchors edges; a raft hides the problem and adds cleanup.
  • Bed temperature up 5°C for the stubborn cases, and clean the plate — grease releases parts as efficiently as heat does.
One change per print. Stringing and warping both respond within a single reprint when you change the right thing — and become mysteries when you change three things at once.

Where these bites are common: tall tree and light-stand silhouettes (warping), and detail-heavy gothic pieces (stringing shows up first on fine ornaments).
